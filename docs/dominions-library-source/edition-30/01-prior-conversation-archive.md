## Scope of this archive

This document consolidates the Dominions material recoverable from earlier conversations and the current TheHoboKingdom repository. It preserves prior ideas and decisions, but it does not assume that every earlier answer was correct.

Three distinctions are used throughout:

- **User-reported** — a game state, preference, decision, or correction supplied directly.
- **Earlier recommendation** — advice previously offered but not necessarily adopted.
- **Verification note** — what is now confirmed, contradicted, incomplete, or still awaiting a test.

## Long-term preferences and goals

The consistent direction has been:

- build a deep Dominions reference rather than a short beginner page;
- favour research-heavy nations and pretenders able to reach major rituals;
- cover Dominions Enhanced and Divinitus, but distinguish them from the unmodded game;
- learn why a plan works, not merely copy a build order;
- publish the results through TheHoboKingdom;
- keep guide prose natural and avoid conspicuous machine-written phrasing;
- use less direct second-person wording in project and strategy prose;
- preserve exact names, paths, costs, turn numbers, and versions;
- verify uncertain mechanics against the manual and practical experience.

## Research-focused nation and pretender work

### Middle Age Arcoscephale

The original preference was a Middle Age nation with excellent research and a god designed around research and high-level rituals. MA Arcoscephale was recommended as the best fit.

Earlier work highlighted:

- Mystics and other inexpensive researchers;
- broad Astral-led magic access;
- communions as a route from modest national mages to high-level battlefield magic;
- a late game based on research speed, forging, path access, globals, and legendary rituals.

Two earlier pretender families were proposed:

1. **Imprisoned Astral–Earth Titan** — S5 E5.
2. **Dormant Great Enchantress** — S4 E4 N3 D2.

The scale packages were not recorded consistently. One recovered version gave the Titan Magic 3, Order 2, and either Production 1 or Growth 1. Another later summary gave a broader ideal of Order 3, Magic 3, Growth 2, Luck 1. The Enchantress was associated with Magic 3, Growth 2, Order 1, Luck 1.

These are historical proposals, not current legal builds. Their design-point costs, chassis paths, scale limits, and bless effects must be rebuilt in the exact active version.

Named ritual goals included:

- Arcane Nexus;
- Master Enslave;
- Wish;
- Forge of the Ancients;
- Tartarian Gate;
- Gift of Health;
- Nature’s Bounty.

Earlier advice sometimes treated these as a common endgame package even when one chassis could not cast all of them unaided. The future guide needs explicit booster, empowerment, communion, transformation, and summoned-mage routes for every target spell.

### Early Age Arcoscephale

After choosing MA Arcoscephale as the research-focused Middle Age option, the same exercise was requested for the Early Age.

The recovered advice emphasised:

- Philosophers as dedicated researchers;
- Mystics for broad magic access;
- an imprisoned Great Sage or Oracle around S5 E4 N2;
- a dormant Great Enchantress around S4 E4 D3 N3 A2;
- scales and research feeding late rituals rather than an immediate rush.

The exact chassis names, point totals, and legal paths need to be reconstructed from the current DE files. No recovered evidence shows that one of these EA builds was finally selected.

### Dominions Enhanced pretender changes discussed

The earlier conversation identified these broad DE changes:

- pretenders generally repriced more cheaply;
- Titans gain two additional paths;
- Monsters and Rainbows gain one;
- Monsters receive general improvements;
- numerous bless costs and Incarnate requirements change.

Some old examples are now stale or were quoted incorrectly. The current DE source lists Wasteland Survival as 2 to 1, while an earlier conversation recorded 5 to 1. Current DE v2.16 also records Flaming Weapons as 7 to 4 and no longer Incarnate; older notes recorded 7 to 6. Versioned changelogs must replace memory-based summaries.

## The mixed-experience multiplayer ruleset

### User-reported setup

- Five human players.
- Two players much more experienced than the other three.
- A large donut map.
- Roughly 25 provinces per player.
- Some ocean provinces.
- Consideration of two AI nations at increased difficulty.
- Dominions Enhanced and Divinitus, described at the time as their latest versions.

### Earlier proposed rules

The following was a proposed package, not a confirmed final ruleset:

- newer players choose nations before the veterans;
- veterans choose last;
- veterans avoid oppressive rush nations, awake expanders, extreme bless rushes, and dominant water starts;
- veterans explain the purpose of their pretender rather than hiding essential setup knowledge;
- add two AI opponents on Normal or Difficult;
- veterans cannot take an AI capital before turn 24 unless that AI attacked them first;
- no human capital siege before turn 18;
- veterans may not exceed 18 provinces by turn 12;
- veterans may not build more than two forts before turn 16;
- formalise NAP terms in writing;
- no military alliance between the veteran players before turn 30;
- no offensive veteran thug or supercombatant use before turn 25;
- no veteran global enchantments before turn 35;
- no repeated remote attacks, assassination campaigns, or similar harassment by veterans before turn 25;
- maintain a shared `#dom6-war-room` advice channel;
- help newer players read battle replays and understand why a fight was lost;
- allow each newer player one mulligan before turn 20.

The strength of this proposal is that it protects learning time without requiring the veterans to make deliberately bad tactical decisions. Its weakness is administrative weight and the possibility that rigid caps distort normal strategy. No recovered message confirms which rules were finally adopted.

- a successful site-search event grants 150 gold and `#1d6vis 51`, meaning 1d6 of every elemental gem;
- teaching events target appropriate Mystics, use candle-based chances, and apply Fire, Water, Earth, and Astral boosts while path requirements enforce the stated caps.

These mechanics are now source-confirmed for Divinitus 1.15.3 DE. Their exact monthly probability behaviour and interactions with Dominions Enhanced still merit an in-game demonstration.

### Game state and strategic pivot

At roughly turn 24:

- Arcoscephale was fighting Ermor and Eriu;
- Tartarian spirits were appearing because another nation’s Hound of Hades had broken free;
- a very strong site had been found that granted an Alteration spell discount;
- the Alteration discount was described as equal in value to the earlier Nexus-related opportunity, although the exact percentage was not recovered.

The plan changed from a Nexus-focused route to:

1. research immediate spells able to answer current threats;
2. rush Wish;
3. use the first Wish on gems to measure the exact result in the active mod combination;
4. use later Wishes to obtain more commanders capable of casting Wish.

Earlier advice had recommended Alteration 9 for Wish followed by Thaumaturgy 9 for Nexus Gate. Once the game pivoted, Nexus Gate was no longer the immediate priority.

### Relevant legendary rituals

The official manual confirms:

- **Wish** — Alteration 9, Astral 9, 100 astral pearls.
- **Nexus Gate** — Thaumaturgy 9, Astral 5 Earth 3, 40 astral pearls; creates a permanent gate to Nexus and cannot be dispelled.
- **Tartarian Gate** — Conjuration 9, Death 7, 7 death gems.
- **Divine Name** — Thaumaturgy 7, Astral 5, 25 astral pearls; can grant a willing target commanding ability and increased mental faculties, or give a mindless being an artificial mind and command.

The previous suggestion to Wish for an Astral-heavy body and then use Divine Name remains a strategy proposal. It does not by itself prove which wished-for units arrive as commanders, which can receive Divine Name, or how modded `NO_WISH` restrictions behave.

## Communion clarification

The user asked whether communions share buffs in the manner of spell singers, then narrowed the question to Regeneration:

- if a communion slave casts Regeneration, does only that slave receive it?
- if a communion master casts it, do the slaves receive it?

The final answer was:

- communions do not share every buff between all participants;
- masters gain path levels from the number of slaves;
- spell fatigue is distributed through the communion;
- slaves cannot act while participating;
- slaves benefit from **self-buffs with range 0 and a single target** cast by communion masters;
- therefore a slave casting Personal Regeneration before joining benefits personally;
- a master casting Personal Regeneration while the communion is active also grants it to the friendly slaves participating in that spell;
- ordinary non-self buffs do not propagate merely because the caster is a master.

This is directly supported by the official manual. The manual also confirms:

- 2 slaves give each master +1 in every path the master already knows;
- 4 slaves give +2;
- fatigue is divided among the participants and then modified by the slave’s relevant path level compared with the master;
- equal path level gives no modifier;
- slave above master halves fatigue;
- slave below master doubles fatigue;
- slave below half the master’s level quadruples fatigue;
- if every master dies or flees, every slave suffers a backlash of approximately one round of stun and 3d50 fatigue per slave.

## AI and diplomacy overhaul

### Final project statement

The user’s retained wording was:

> My goal is to make Dominions 6 multiplayer feel less like we are just fighting random nations and more like we are playing in a living world where every neighbour is watching what you do. If you expand too hard, claim too many thrones, push your dominion into someone’s borders, or act like a warmonger, the other nations should react with warnings, grudges, raids, defensive pacts, and eventually full coalitions. I probably can’t rewrite the AI’s brain completely, but Im gonna try build a diplomacy and event system around it that makes the world push back in a way that feels believable.

An assistant rewrite changed the collective wording to I/my throughout. The user chose to keep the paragraph above.

### Design inputs

The project began with the intention of providing manuals and YouTube transcripts.

The first identified transcript was:

**“HOW TO START — Full Tutorial Gameplay Guide DOMINIONS 6 Ep 1”**

Recovered scenario details included:

- Early Age;
- a nation recorded only as “M” in the recovered context;
- Easy AI;
- Keeper of the Bridge pretender;
- Thrones of Ascension victory;
- early scouting, recruitment, Enchantment 3 research, and end-turn fundamentals.

The design lessons drawn from the early transcripts were:

- each nation needs its own doctrine rather than a universal AI build;
- expansion failure is the first problem to address;
- pretender design is a major AI weakness;
- research can be influenced through `#researchgoal`;
- ritual and forging priorities can be influenced through `#favrit`;
- direct control over tactical scripting is limited;
- diplomacy should react to borders, thrones, visible strength, and perceived danger.

Later diplomacy synthesis focused on:

- land, position, gold, resources, gems, and thrones as reasons for war;
- scouting and information as the basis for opportunity;
- non-binding diplomacy and NAPs, often using a three-turn expiry;
- a hidden “book of grudges”;
- border legitimacy;
- reputation for warmongering;
- warnings and retaliatory raids;
- defensive alignment against a runaway;
- coalition pressure that changes the whole table.

Two illustrative diplomatic personalities were proposed:

- **fair border** — recognises claims and responds predictably to trespass;
- **warmonger** — sees weakness and opportunity as reasons to push.

### Confirmed modding tools

The modding manual confirms nation-specific AI templates with:

- `#newtemplate`;
- `#form`;
- `#prison`;
- `#magic`;
- `#domstr`;
- `#scale`;
- `#bless`;
- `#researchgoal`;
- `#favrit`.

The event manual confirms special province event orders:

| Response | Bitmask | Target-order number |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Investigate | 1 | 100 |
| Continue | 2 | 101 |
| Accept | 4 | 102 |
| Decline | 8 | 103 |
| Withdraw | 16 | not listed as a target order |
| Attack | 32 | 105 |
| Diplomacy | 64 | 106 |
| Subterfuge | 128 | 107 |
| Magic | 256 | 108 |

`#order` receives the sum of the desired bitmasks. Every listed response totals 511; all responses except Investigate and Continue total 508. Earlier discussion used 508 for the incident-response set.

`#req_targorder` can identify a commander performing one of the listed event orders. Event codes, delays, province flags, and variables can construct follow-up chains.

This supports interactive incidents. It does not prove that the hard-coded diplomacy screen or AI reasoning can be rewritten.

### Formation question

After putting the larger project on hold, the user asked whether new formations could be added on top of the game’s basic options.

The recovered answer identified:

- Box;
- Line;
- Double Line;
- Sparse Line;
- Skirmish.

The ordinary `.dm` interface does not expose a command for creating another selectable formation button. Relevant unit commands include:

- `#formationfighter <xsize>` — allows more size points to fit in a square;
- `#undisciplined` — restricts the unit to Skirmish and removes normal battle orders;
- `#skirmisher` — changes the unit’s skirmishing behaviour.

New formation behaviour can therefore be imitated through unit density, discipline, commander design, placement, and scenario-specific troops, but it cannot currently be presented as a genuinely new player-selectable formation.

The project remained paused after this question.
